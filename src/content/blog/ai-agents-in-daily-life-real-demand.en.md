The Reddit headline is blunt: **Basically no one is using AI agents for their DoorDash orders.**

Thousands of upvotes. The comments are blunter: why would anyone use AI to order food—or to order anything already two taps away?

Source: [r/technology](https://www.reddit.com/r/technology/comments/1xl21ad/basically_no_one_is_using_ai_agents_for_their/)

## What people are actually rejecting

![AI 接入生活是真需求吗：没人用 Agent 点外卖，千问烧钱也难买习惯 — overview](../../assets/inline-ai-agents-in-daily-life-real-demand-01.jpg)

Not “AI is useless.” **Wrong surface area.**

Food apps spent a decade compressing browse → cart → pay → track. An agent that “orders for you” rarely saves time or money; it adds ambiguity, wrong items, and payment risk. Top take: AI on a simple task is unnecessary complexity.

Worse foil: an exec auto-buys vinyl via Claude and asks if AI could play board games for him. The thread treats that as a symptom—how much of living do you want outsourced?

Capital narrative matters too: firms mortgaged the future on “agents everywhere,” so the story must look alive. Useful niches (coding, long workflows) get inflated into a total life OS—hence the joke about a trillion dollars that still can’t coordinate 500 Chalupas at 3:47 a.m.

## Demand has layers

| Layer | Examples | Agent-shaped? |
|---|---|---|
| High cognitive friction | Coding, multi-tool workflows | Often yes |
| Medium decisions | Research, drafting, comparison | Sometimes |
| Low-friction commerce | Food delivery, tickets, familiar apps | Usually no—UI already won |

The test: **after novelty and subsidies, do people still open it, finish the same job faster/safer/calmer than the old path?**

If not, it’s a demo, not demand.

## China mirror: Qwen-class apps

Domestic narrative is louder: native AI apps as super-entry; Alibaba’s Qwen pushes “get things done in one sentence”—milk tea, delivery, tickets—via paid growth and holiday free-order blitzes. Public reports show downloads and holiday DAU can be bought into impressive charts; some claim sticky post-holiday multiples and huge MAU spikes.

The same coverage keeps warning:

- Money buys pulse traffic, not guaranteed retention when red packets stop  
- Many users admit the draw was subsidies; AI usage drops when work contexts fade  
- Even strong MAU later can cool off peaks—growth myths ate low bases and campaign juice

So China doesn’t refute Reddit—it adds: **you can purchase a first “agent ordered for me” moment; you rarely purchase “I’ll always ask the agent” habit.**

Doubao leans content/multimodal; Qwen leans transaction loops. Shared lesson: entry wars buy awareness; retention needs irreplaceable pain. Industry notes also show plugin-shaped AI users often dwarf native AI apps—people want capability inside existing flows, not another master brain.

## Product read

![AI 接入生活是真需求吗：没人用 Agent 点外卖，千问烧钱也难买习惯 — detail](../../assets/inline-ai-agents-in-daily-life-real-demand-02.jpg)

1. **Tool-use ≠ permission to act.** Control, correction cost, and payment risk dominate life tasks.  
2. **Agents should attack friction apps haven’t killed**—not friction already dead.  
3. **Paid growth validates marketing and ecosystem leverage, not PMF.** Free orders prove checkout works; repeat unpaid opens prove life needs it.  
4. **Exec taste ≠ mass taste.** Vinyl auto-buyers and “feed me in five minutes” users are different markets.

## Bottom line

“AI in daily life” can be a real direction. **“Every life task should go through an agent” is mostly fake demand**—at least for DoorDash-class jobs, global comment sections and China’s spend wars rhyme: people want short, certain paths—not a talkative middleman.

Put agents where complexity earns the proxy. Entries can be bought. Habits must be earned.
