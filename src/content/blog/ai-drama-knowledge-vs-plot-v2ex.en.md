> Source: [I forced myself to watch AI short dramas for half an hour+](https://www.v2ex.com/t/1256754) (V2EX, 94 replies)

The OP chased podcast/X hype into *Armed Into Song* and *Wan Yao Tu Lu Zhuan*. Bad frames and toilet-paper plots were acceptable — **immersion still failed**. His diagnosis: wrong performance rhythm, like those childhood TV shows that felt off until you learned the acting was bad.

The real question: **do AI dramas have performance rhythm at all?**

## What the thread argued

![AI dramas: dopamine lane vs shot language](../../assets/inline-ai-drama-knowledge-vs-plot-v2ex-01-en.jpg)

### “Don’t bring a brain” (loudest)

Short drama as electronic snack, brain massage, rice-companion dopamine. Scrutiny kills it; the job is emptying the mind — almost meditation. Pop songs as analogy: lyrics don’t survive close reading; the hook is the product. Taste rarely downsizes: if you watch with anime/film habits, you won’t get the hit.

### Directing / pacing camp (with the OP)

Jump cuts break blocking and costumes; everyone shouts lines; no breath, no subtext; episode length chops thought mid-sentence. The OP keeps repeating: he doesn’t need depth or zero glitches — **the same beat still has to be told with shots**. Blame isn’t only “AI is weak”; many makers lack basic directing/writing craft.

### What works is often not pure “爽”

Some say gun-crossing power fantasies fail, but **industry-insider** AI shows are watchable. History-completion pieces get praise for teaching while entertaining. Others bet on educational immersion — information density first; audiences are less vicious about micro-expressions and causal gaps.

### Transition era vs taste wall

One side expects higher barriers + better models → boutique quality. Another says polished storyboards already exist, but they’re a different river from mass dopamine shows. Recs splinter hard — proof “AI short drama” isn’t one category.

## My take

![Explainers pass; plot detail fails](../../assets/inline-ai-drama-knowledge-vs-plot-v2ex-02-en.jpg)

**This generation of AI video fits knowledge explainers; plot work still can’t survive detail scrutiny.**

- **Why explainers pass**: viewers buy structure — process, principle, contrast, timeline. Picture aids memory; you don’t need to trust the next breath. Glitches hurt polish, not belief.
- **Why plot shatters**: stories need continuity — who stands where, eye-line subtext, rest between peaks, cause you can follow. AI shorts jump, freeze, overfill, and fake exactly there. Formula thrills can clone web novels; **performance density can’t clone a good director**.
- **Same split as the thread**: watchable “industry / history” pieces sit near explainers; drop-offs sit in narrative performance. You didn’t refuse to drop your brain — wrong shot rhythm kept kicking you out.

Models may fix micro-expression and consistency later. Until then, treating AI video as a tool to **make one thing clear** is more honest — and a better product bet — than forcing it into serialized drama.
