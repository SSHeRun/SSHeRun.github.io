People keep asking me: how do you cold-start?

The most effective approach costs nothing.

This isn't something only broke founders do. It's what people who truly understand how PMF happens actually do. AFFiNE co-founder Iris shared two years of overseas go-to-market experience on a podcast: 1,000 GitHub stars in 72 hours after open-sourcing, 10,000 in 43 days, users across 200+ countries. Here are her core cold-start methods.

![Global entrepreneurs connecting on a digital map](../../assets/inline-overseas-product-zero-cost-cold-start-01.jpg)

## Two Cases That Wake You Up

**HeyGen's early days:** they opened a "video studio" on Fiverr — and used it to run **1,800 user interviews**. 1,800.

**Lark in Southeast Asia:** of the Malaysia team's first 50 customers, 30 came from LinkedIn DMs. Not ads. Not KOLs. Twenty manual messages a day.

The lesson: cold start isn't waiting for users. It's going out to talk to people.

## 10 Zero-Cost Ways to Get Early Users

### 1. Your Own Network

Get over the awkwardness. Ask people you already know. Your first users are often in your contacts.

### 2. DM Competitor Followers

People following competitors on Twitter or LinkedIn are potential users. They're already interested in the category.

### 3. LinkedIn Micro-Targeting

Target regular people, not influencers. An influencer's inbox has 100 unread messages; a regular person's might have one — reply rates are completely different.

### 4. Reddit Keyword Search

Search for pain points you solve, then reply with genuine value. Pro tip: lurk for 1-2 weeks before posting. Reddit has real users, hard to buy traffic, but trust is durable once earned.

### 5. Hacker News Show HN

A lottery ticket — hit the front page and you get hundreds of stars. Don't rely on it alone, but worth trying.

### 6. Product Hunt

ROI is declining, but new products can still try. Category matters — tech audiences bring real users; non-tech can still get a ranking boost.

### 7. Fiverr / Upwork

Spend a little to hire people for research and contact finding. HeyGen's 1,800 interviews came from this path.

### 8. Events

Meetups, hackathons, online or offline. Five minutes face-to-face beats 100 DMs.

### 9. Complementary Product Partnerships

Find projects with overlapping but non-competing users. Cross-promote at zero cost. Both sides win because incremental users cost nothing.

### 10. Cold Email

Personalize. Show you've read their content. Mass templates = spam.

## Execution: Combine, Don't Spray

Don't look "busy" across 8 platforms simultaneously. Pick 3-5 methods and execute.

- 10 messages with no reply? The method isn't wrong — your **sample size is too small**
- Send 100, then go deep with 5 people
- Define your first wave of paying-intent users, find where they hang out, go there

Same principle as [[first-principles-startup-review|first-principles startup review]]: don't cast a wide net — go all-in on one channel to get your first 100 paying users.

## The Hidden Community Cold-Start Trick

Iris shared something easy to miss: **strangers in a community don't talk to each other.**

The fix: have a real person (support) DM new members — thank them for joining, share a guide and useful links, offer help anytime. If someone chats 5-6 screens with support, schedule a meeting. Trust in the person transfers to trust in the product, and they start speaking up in the community.

## Three Mindsets for Small Overseas Teams

**First, aesthetics differ wildly by country.** toC products need real localization, not just translation.

**Second, before $1M ARR, focus on toC + PLG.** Get VOC (voice of customer) through organic growth — listen to needs, don't show muscle.

**Third, not knowing overseas channels is the biggest trap.** Twitter, Reddit, Medium, Hacker News, Discord, Slack, Telegram are all mature. Lurk in competitor communities and study their playbooks frame by frame.

## There Is No Shortcut — and That's the Shortcut

Combine 3-5 methods. Send 100 DMs. Go deep with 5 people.

Most people hear "there's no shortcut" and immediately go looking for one anyway. That's the information asymmetry — the people actually willing to do the work are fewer than you think.
