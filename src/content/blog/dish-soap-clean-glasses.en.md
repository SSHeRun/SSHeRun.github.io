Someone saw their manager washing glasses with dish soap. Zeiss lenses cost real money—would long-term washing ruin the coatings?

On V2EX, about a hundred replies turned “is this allowed?” into “this is the correct method.”

Source: [V2EX #1254628](https://www.v2ex.com/t/1254628)

## Bottom line

![洗洁精洗眼镜靠谱吗 — overview](../../assets/inline-dish-soap-clean-glasses-01.jpg)

**Neutral dish soap is the recommended path, not a hack.** Zeiss’s own cleaning guide, opticians, and Essilor support replies in the thread converge: rinse dust first, foam second, rinse again, avoid dry rubbing.

The haze you blame on detergent is often **micro-sandpaper**: wiping grit that was never rinsed off.

## What the thread argued

1. Dish soap is fine—and frequently “most recommended”
2. Clear water must remove grit before any wipe
3. Cloths/tissues used dry are the main damage vector
4. Resin lenses are consumables (~2–3 years); transmission drops anyway
5. Shop ultrasonic baths get blamed by some for scratches

Body wash/shampoo split the room. Alcohol/IPA too: loved by some, coating-risky for others, especially photochromic lenses.

## A low-scratch routine

![洗洁精洗眼镜靠谱吗 — detail](../../assets/inline-dish-soap-clean-glasses-02.jpg)

1. High flow rinse both sides  
2. Neutral dish soap foam on wet fingers  
3. Gentle two-finger pass—no pressure grinding  
4. Rinse clean  
5. Sheet of fine water to carry droplets off hydrophobic coat, or **dab** with tissue  
6. Nose pads may green from trapped water—cheap to replace

Many “lens sprays” reportedly smell like… dish soap.

## Three takeaways

1. Even expensive Zeiss glass follows the dish-soap manual  
2. KPIs are dust → oil → minimal friction, not fancier cloths  
3. Budget lenses as consumables; clarity from replacement often beats museum-grade babysitting

The expensive move isn’t the pump of detergent. It’s the one hard wipe with grit still on the lens.
