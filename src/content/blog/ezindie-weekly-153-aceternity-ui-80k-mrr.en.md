[ezindie indie monetization weekly issue #153](https://www.ezindie.com/weekly/issue-153) leads with a blunt headline: **a website UI component library making $80,000 a month.**

Among five products in the issue, **Aceternity UI** founder Manu's story is the one worth unpacking. The other four are useful radar.

## The main story: hobby components to six-figure months

Manu is a developer who also runs design studio **Aceternity**, building sites for founders. The component library started as a side effect of personal projects.

### The pivot: nobody reads tutorials, everyone wants copy-paste

He blogged about *how* to build flashy components. Analytics said readers did not want the journey — they wanted the code.

So the playbook changed:

- Stop being a "cheap substitute for a tutorial"
- Ship a simple site over a weekend (Next.js, Tailwind, Framer Motion)
- Launch with **only 7 components** — lessons from over-polishing products nobody used

Hosting cost: about **$20/mo on Vercel**.

### Growth flywheel

1. **Product Hunt + Twitter/X** for first users  
2. **Fireship** and similar channels → global inbound  
3. ~4 months later: site as distribution, **20K Twitter followers**  
4. Launch **Aceternity UI Pro** on that base — lower friction  
5. Primary channel stays **word of mouth** — users recommend it at work and on social

First customer arrived days after launch. Early client John Shahawy (a founder) became a two-year+ partner and referral engine.

### Revenue and team (last month)

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| UI Pro subscriptions | **~$40K** |
| Custom web builds | **~$30K** |
| Total monthly revenue | **$60–100K** |
| Monthly spend | **~$5K** |

Team of **6** (2 frontend, 1 full-stack, 1 designer, 1 social, 1 PM). Manu once juggled **7 clients** at once — hired full-time help for delivery, then scaled. Focus now: **UI Pro**, SEO, and content (YouTube + X).

Pro revenue passed **$80K in just two months** — the number behind this week's title.

## Five takeaways for indie builders

1. **Distribution beats perfection** — 7 components is enough to test demand; ship, then stack. Manu's line: bad code beats overthinking; customers do not care about code quality.  
2. **Match content to behavior** — tutorials did not convert; copy-paste code did.  
3. **Free tier for reputation, Pro for revenue** — services can run in parallel, but scale lives in repeatable product.  
4. **Show up authentically** — share wins, losses, and process; the community will push you.  
5. **Hiring signal** — when client load breaks you, delegate.

## What's next for them

Manu targets **$100K MRR** for Pro next year and is testing ads and YouTube. For anyone copying this path, the hard part is rarely "can I write components" — it's **shipping 7 components first, staying visible on X, and gradually trading service revenue for product revenue**.
